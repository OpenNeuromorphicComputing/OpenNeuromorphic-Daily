### [What Is the Dynamic Capacitance of a Memristor?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00849v1)
**2026-10-31** | *Juan Bisquert, Gonzalo Rivera-Sierra*

> Impedance spectroscopy of memristors, nanofluidic channels and halide perovskite devices often shows a low-frequency capacitance that is orders of magnitude larger than any plausible stored charge, or that becomes negative and appears together with an inductive loop. We distinguish two kinds of capacitance that enter the impedance in the same way: a storage capacitance, the derivative of a stored charge, and a dynamic capacitance, produced by the delayed response of a conduction current. Using the conductance-activated quasi-linear memristor (CALM) model, and a general memristor in which an internal state controls the conductance, we show that the dynamic capacitance is the time integral of the transient differential conductance after a small voltage step, measured from its final value. The same picture accounts for the capacitive and inductive regimes of halide perovskite memristors.
