### [Migrate Computation, Not Data: Fault Recovery in Neuromorphic Many-Core Systems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26985v1)
**2026-10-19** | *Muyang Cao, Di Yu, Peiling Zhu et al.*

> As neuromorphic systems scale to thousands of near-memory cores, aging-induced permanent faults in neuron-update datapaths become an important obstacle to long-term deployment. Existing online recovery methods typically retire a faulty core and migrate its neurons, along with their weights and membrane states, to healthy cores. This whole-core treatment incurs high recovery traffic and makes recovery depend on scarce destinations; at high utilization, there may be enough surviving compute capacity but insufficient free storage to copy the affected neuron context. This paper presents SpikeRelay, an online recovery framework for diagnosed compute-unit faults. SpikeRelay exploits the fact that, in many neuromorphic cores, the compute datapath, local SRAM, and router are distinct modules: a compute fault need not destroy stored weights and states or disconnect the core from the NoC.
