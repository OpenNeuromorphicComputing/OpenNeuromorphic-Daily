### [Precursor Ratio-Controlled Chloride Vacancies in Lead-Free KCuCl3-x Memristors for Forming-Free Gradual Resistive Switching and Neuromorphic Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35436v1)
**2026-10-26** | *Chinmayee Mandar Mhaskar, Sangeeta Roy Chaudhuri, Ayan Roy Chaudhuri*

> Defect engineering of memristive materials offers a powerful strategy for regulating ionic transport and analog resistive switching required for neuromorphic computing. Here, we report a wet chemical synthesis route for chloride-vacancy (VCl) engineering in lead-free all-inorganic KCuCl3-x thin films and establish a direct correlation between defect concentration, electronic structure, transport dynamics, and neuromorphic performance. Controlled precursor-ratio variation systematically tunes the VCl concentration in KCuCl3-x thin films, driving preferential vacancy formation at the terminal chloride sites, band-gap narrowing, and enhanced electronic disorder. ### [Migrate Computation, Not Data: Fault Recovery in Neuromorphic Many-Core Systems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26985v1)
**2026-10-19** | *Muyang Cao, Di Yu, Peiling Zhu et al.*

> As neuromorphic systems scale to thousands of near-memory cores, aging-induced permanent faults in neuron-update datapaths become an important obstacle to long-term deployment. Existing online recovery methods typically retire a faulty core and migrate its neurons, along with their weights and membrane states, to healthy cores. This whole-core treatment incurs high recovery traffic and makes recovery depend on scarce destinations; at high utilization, there may be enough surviving compute capacity but insufficient free storage to copy the affected neuron context. This paper presents SpikeRelay, an online recovery framework for diagnosed compute-unit faults. SpikeRelay exploits the fact that, in many neuromorphic cores, the compute datapath, local SRAM, and router are distinct modules: a compute fault need not destroy stored weights and states or disconnect the core from the NoC. Instead of retiring a faulty core and migrating its neuron data, SpikeRelay converts the faulty core into a storage-and-forwarding node while offloading neuron-update computation to a healthy rescue core. This decoupling of storage and computation eliminates recovery-time data migration and greatly reduces spare-SRAM requirements. Consequently, SpikeRelay sustains 100\% recovery at 92.2\% SRAM utilization and a 50\% fault rate, whereas the best baseline achieves only 22.1\%.
